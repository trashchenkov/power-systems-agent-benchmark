2. **Hard constraints**:
   - Volt-VAR curve voltage_pu breakpoints must be strictly increasing
   - Volt-VAR curve q_fraction values must be monotonic non-increasing as 
voltage rises
   - Reactive power command at each timestep must stay within inverter

   - Corrected voltage at each timestep must remain within voltage_limits_pu

3. **Public inputs**: 3 timesteps with different base voltages and conditions

Let me calculate the required reactive power to bring each timestep within 
voltage limits:

For each timestep, the corrected voltage = base_voltage_pu + 
voltage_sensitivity_pu_per_kvar * q_kvar

Where q_kvar = q_fraction * inverter_kva (since q_fraction is normalized by kVA)

The inverter reactive power capability is sqrt(kVA^2 - kW^2).

Let me compute the required q_kvar for each timestep to hit the voltage limits:

**Timestep t_low** (base=0.94, needs to increase voltage to at least 0.95):
- Need to raise voltage by at least 0.01 pu
- q_kvar ≥ 0.01 / 0.0007 ≈ 14.29 kvar
- Inverter capability: sqrt(100^2 - 60^2) = sqrt(6400) = 80 kvar
- So q_fraction ≥ 14.29/100 = 0.1429

**Timestep t_nom** (base=1.0, already within limits 0.95-1.05):
- Can use minimal reactive power, but need to maintain monotonic curve

**Timestep t_high** (base=1.07, needs to decrease voltage to at most 1.05):
- Need to lower voltage by at least 0.02 pu
- q_kvar ≤ -0.02 / 0.0007 ≈ -28.57 kvar
- So q_fraction ≤ -28.57/100 = -0.2857

For a Volt-VAR curve that is monotonic non-increasing (q_fraction decreases as 
voltage increases), I need:
- At low voltage: higher q_fraction (positive, injecting reactive power to raise
voltage)
- At high voltage: lower q_fraction (negative, absorbing reactive power to lower
voltage)

A simple approach is to use a 2-point curve:
- Point at 0.95 pu: q_fraction = 0.2 (injects 20 kvar)
- Point at 1.05 pu: q_fraction = -0.3 (absorbs 30 kvar)
